# What is VoxGarden?

## One sentence

VoxGarden is a platform that lets ordinary people run their own internet radio station cheaply and without technical knowledge, and lets listeners find those stations on a map of the world.

## The problem

Starting a traditional radio station needs a licence, a transmitter, a studio and staff. Plenty of people with good ideas never get a microphone: a presenter whose employer limits what they can say, a student with no campus station, a community with its own language and nobody covering it.

Internet radio removes the transmitter. But the tools that exist (Radio.co, Shoutcast hosting, self-run servers) are either priced in dollars, or built for people who already know what an encoder and a stream password are. The gap is not technology. It is price, setup and being found.

## What we are building

Four products that share one account, one station identity and one media library.

### 1. VoxGarden Create (phone)
Record, trim, add an intro and jingle, clean the volume, then tap **Send to my station**. It works on a phone you already own and does basic editing on the device so it does not burn data. First version is a web app that installs like an app (PWA), tested on cheap Android phones before we consider native apps.

### 2. VoxGarden Studio (web)
Sign up, name your station, upload a logo. We set up everything behind the scenes. Then: upload audio, schedule shows, invite co-presenters, press **Go Live** from the browser. Listeners get a public station page and a share link.

### 3. VoxGarden Engine (invisible)
Streaming, scheduled playback (AutoDJ), storage and listener delivery. We start from AzuraCast, an open-source radio suite, and hide it entirely behind our own interface. Users never see its settings, passwords or API.

### 4. VoxGarden Globe (listeners)
A spinning globe, inspired by Radio Garden. Pick a place, hear a live station. Every published VoxGarden station appears on it. Listening needs no account.

## Who it is for

- Independent broadcasters and presenters
- Schools and universities (campus stations and journalism training)
- Communities and language groups
- Creators in music, faith, sport, business, education
- Experienced broadcasters who want independence

## How it earns money

| Plan | Who | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| Community (free) | Beginners | One station, small storage, basic automation, listed on the Globe |
| Independent | Serious solo broadcasters | More storage, analytics, extra presenters |
| Professional | Commercial stations, media teams | Teams, ad tools, branded player |
| Campus | Schools and universities | Student accounts, oversight, training material |

Price points are hypotheses to test with real broadcasters, not decisions. The hard rule: **never promise unlimited listeners at a flat price**. Bandwidth and storage cost real money, so plans scale with usage.

Later revenue: sponsorship and advert tools, podcast distribution, paid training.

## Why a partnership

Three founders, each owning a lane (suggested, to be agreed): Ayobami on partnerships and broadcaster relationships, Bamidele on product and audio infrastructure, Motunrayo on community, creators and education. Before real money or code is involved we write a founders' agreement covering ownership, contribution, IP, pay, exits and how we settle disagreements. Friendship is the reason to do this properly, not an excuse to skip it.

## What success looks like

- A first-time broadcaster goes live without spending a fortune or asking for help.
- A university's students get real on-air experience.
- A community is heard by people outside it.
- A presenter builds an audience, earns, and hires someone.
- The three of us run a sustainable company that creates openings for others.

## What we do not know yet

Whether people will pay, what a listener actually costs us, what Nigerian regulation requires of an internet-only station, whether our name can be protected, and how well browser-based live broadcasting behaves on weak connections. [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md) lists each of these and how we find out.
